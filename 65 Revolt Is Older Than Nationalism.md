## Before

Seasons 2–8 followed empires and regions, and revolt appeared throughout. Season 9 turns resistance and decolonization into the main subject. It begins by showing that armed refusal is older than the nation-state.

## The turn

In November 1780 José Gabriel Condorcanqui, a *kuraka* (Indigenous lord) of Tinta near Cusco who claimed descent from the last Inca, took the name Túpac Amaru II and seized and executed the local *corregidor*. The rebellion targeted the *reparto de mercancías*, the mita, and abusive officials (Episode 42). It spread across the southern Andes, and in Upper Peru (Bolivia) Túpac Katari besieged La Paz in 1781. Micaela Bastidas, Túpac Amaru’s wife, was a commander and strategist. Spanish forces captured and executed Túpac Amaru, Bastidas, and their family in Cusco in May 1781. The rebellion and its repression killed tens of thousands — an estimate often given as around 100,000, which remains uncertain. Afterward Spain abolished the *reparto* and suppressed Inca noble titles and symbols.

The 1857 rebellion in northern India appears here only as a comparative slot, already limited by Episode 21: a rising that began among soldiers and drew in peasants and dispossessed rulers. Its history belongs to *Discovery of India*. In October 1865 at Morant Bay, Jamaica, Paul Bogle led a protest over poverty, land, and justice that turned into a rising. Governor Edward Eyre declared martial law. Troops killed more than 400 people, hundreds more were flogged, and George William Gordon, a mixed-race legislator, was hanged after a court-martial. The controversy in Britain divided intellectuals, with John Stuart Mill against Eyre and Thomas Carlyle for him. Jamaica’s assembly surrendered its charter, and the island became a Crown colony (Episode 22). The Herero and Nama war (Episode 30) and Bambatha (Episode 55) belong in this lineage and are not retold.

## Acts

### 1. What revolt wanted

A method act: pre-nationalist revolts often demanded justice under the existing order, restoration of rights, or removal of abusive officials, rather than a nation-state. Revolt is categorized by demand, not by later national memory.

### 2. Túpac Amaru II and Túpac Katari

The rebellion’s causes, spread, and leadership, including Micaela Bastidas and Bartolina Sisa. Its repression and consequences. Estimated tolls labeled. Later memory: Peru’s 1968 military government made Túpac Amaru a national symbol.

### 3. 1857, as a comparative slot

A short act placing 1857 beside other revolts by type: a soldiers’ rising, with peasant and elite participation, against a company-state. Then the handoff card to *Discovery of India*.

### 4. Morant Bay

Paul Bogle, George William Gordon, and Governor Eyre. The martial law killings with figures from the Royal Commission (1866). The Eyre controversy in Britain. Bogle and Gordon as National Heroes of Jamaica.

### 5. A lineage, not a reel

A comparative board: Túpac Amaru, 1857, Morant Bay, Herero, Maji Maji, and Bambatha. Demands, leadership, repression, and consequences. The act refuses a montage of atrocities and keeps to analysis.

## Comparative note

Enslaved revolts (Episodes 38–39) and maroon wars (Episode 43) are part of the same long history of armed refusal. Nationalism later claimed these revolts as ancestors, sometimes accurately and sometimes by rewriting their aims.

## How we know

Spanish colonial trial records of the Túpac Amaru rebellion (Archivo General de Indias; published *Colección Documental*). The Jamaica Royal Commission report (1866). Colonial military and inquiry records. Scholarship by Charles Walker (*The Tupac Amaru Rebellion*, 2014), Sinclair Thomson (*We Alone Will Rule*, 2002), Gad Heuman (*“The Killing Time”*, 1994), and Catherine Hall on the Eyre controversy.

## Do not claim

- That these revolts were nationalist movements in the modern sense.
- That the Túpac Amaru death toll is known precisely.
- That 1857 is narrated here; it is a comparative slot only.
- That colonial repression was proportionate or restrained.
- That revolt was futile because it was defeated; each changed colonial policy.
