## Before

The viewer arrives with two ready-made pictures. In one, brave navigators “discover” new worlds and bring progress. In the other, a single evil called colonialism fell on passive victims. Both pictures are simple, and both are wrong. Both also begin with 1492, as if domination began on a beach in the Caribbean.

## The turn

The Latin *colonia* meant a settlement of Roman citizens, often veterans, on land taken from others. Greek *apoikia* and Phoenician settlements are older still. Conquest, tribute, forced labor, and settlement on other people’s land were not invented in the Atlantic. Mexica, Inca, Ming, Mughal, Ottoman, Asante, and Russian rulers all took land and tribute before or alongside the western European empires.

What was new after the 15th century was a combination. Monarchs claimed oceans and distant coasts by treaty and papal bull. Chartered companies became armed sovereigns. The Atlantic plantation depended on enslaved Africans carried across an ocean. Plants, animals, and diseases moved between continents that had been separate for millennia. By the 19th century a few states held formal sovereignty over most of the world’s land surface. The series films that combination as a system: ships, companies, plantations, missions, law, and guns. It films just as closely the people who survived it, bent it, revolted against it, and ended much of it.

## After

Episode 2 shows the world the ships entered: full of states, markets, and routes that owed nothing to Europe.

## Acts

### 1. A Roman word

An inscription naming a Roman *colonia* (for example, a veterans’ colony in Gaul or Spain) and a surveyor’s grid (centuriation) seen from the air. The act establishes that “colony” first meant land for settlers taken from others. Then it leaves Rome. This series is not a history of the ancient world.

### 2. The word goes to sea

Iberian, English, French, and Dutch legal usage from the 16th and 17th centuries. The word stretched to cover things that looked nothing like Roman farms: a fort on the Gold Coast, a sugar island worked by enslaved people, a company’s factory in Surat, a settler town in Massachusetts. The act shows the word covering very different arrangements. Episode 5 sorts them.

### 3. What was new

Four new things, each tied to a later season: oceanic legal claims (Episode 3), chartered companies (Episode 4 and Season 3), the Atlantic plantation and the slave trade (Season 5), and ecological transfer between continents (Episode 41). Each is shown with one object: a copy of the Treaty of Tordesillas, a VOC share certificate, a plantation ledger, a potato.

### 4. What was not new

Tribute empires already present when Europeans arrived: the Triple Alliance collecting tribute recorded in the *Codex Mendoza*, itself a colonial-era copy made for a Spanish viceroy. The act uses this document to show how even the evidence for pre-colonial empire passes through colonial hands.

### 5. The two false pictures

The act takes apart both the discovery story and the pure-victim story. It closes with the series’ working thesis: colonial power was a world system that remade maps, labor, and ecologies. It was met by survival, adaptation, revolt, and decolonization. Decolonization did not arrive on one day and did not finish.

## Comparative note

The Ottoman, Qing, and Mughal empires expanded in the same centuries. This series names them so viewers can see that western European overseas empire was one form of empire among several, not the only one. It does not claim they were colonies of Europe or that they were the same kind of power (Episode 31).

## How we know

Latin epigraphy and Roman land-survey texts for the word’s origin. Early modern legal and commercial vocabulary in Spanish, Portuguese, English, Dutch, and French. The *Codex Mendoza* (Bodleian Library, MS. Arch. Selden. A. 1). The historiography of empire as a global system, from world-systems approaches to newer histories of empire that stress negotiation and limits.

## Do not claim

- That 1492 invented conquest, slavery, or empire.
- That “colony” meant the same thing in Rome, Barbados, Batavia, and Algiers.
- That the *Codex Mendoza* is a pre-conquest Mexica document; it was painted under Spanish rule, c. 1540s.
- That colonized people were only victims, or that colonial rule was a gift of progress.
- That the series will cover every empire in history; it follows the western European overseas empires and their direct peers (Japan, the United States, Russia) from the 15th century.
