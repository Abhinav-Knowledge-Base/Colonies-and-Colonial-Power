## Before

Episode 29 showed a king’s colony in Central Africa. Germany, unified in 1871, acquired colonies from 1884: Southwest Africa, Togo, Cameroon, East Africa, and Pacific islands.

## The turn

In German Southwest Africa (now Namibia), settlers took Herero and Nama land and cattle. In January 1904 the Herero under Samuel Maharero rose against German rule. After the battle of the Waterberg (August 1904), many Herero fled into the Omaheke desert. On 2 October 1904 General Lothar von Trotha issued an order stating that every Herero within German borders, with or without a rifle, would be shot. The order was later modified under pressure from Berlin, but the policy of driving people into the desert had already taken effect. The Nama under Hendrik Witbooi and others rose in October 1904. Survivors of both peoples were imprisoned in concentration camps, including on Shark Island at Lüderitz, where mortality was extreme. Estimates suggest that up to about 80 percent of the Herero and about half the Nama died. These are estimates from uncertain pre-war population figures.

In German East Africa, the Maji Maji war (1905–07) united many peoples against forced cotton cultivation, using a sacred water (*maji*) believed to protect fighters. German forces used scorched-earth tactics, and famine followed. Estimates of deaths range widely, from about 75,000 to 300,000, most from famine. In Cameroon, the Duala leader Rudolf Duala Manga Bell was executed in 1914 after protesting land expropriation. Togo was promoted in Germany as a “model colony,” a label contradicted by forced labor and taxation.

In 2021 Germany and Namibia issued a joint declaration in which Germany acknowledged the events of 1904–08 as genocide “from today’s perspective” and pledged development funds. Many Herero and Nama representatives rejected the agreement because they were not directly party to it and because it was not framed as reparations.

## Acts

### 1. Settlers and land

German Southwest Africa’s settler economy, land and cattle loss, and the protection treaties that Herero and Nama leaders had signed and found worthless.

### 2. 1904

The Herero rising and the Nama rising as wars fought by Africans with strategy and leadership. Samuel Maharero and Hendrik Witbooi. Africans fought; they were not only victims.

### 3. The order and the camps

Von Trotha’s order, read once as a document. The desert, the camps, and Shark Island. The camera stays on documents and empty landscapes. No images of the dead. Skulls taken to Germany for racial research are named as a restitution issue; returns began in 2011. No remains are shown.

### 4. Maji Maji

The war’s causes, its religious dimension, and its spread across peoples. The German response and the famine. Death estimates shown as a range.

### 5. Cameroon, Togo, and after

Rudolf Duala Manga Bell’s execution, and the “model colony” label examined. Germany lost its colonies in 1919 to League of Nations mandates. The 2021 declaration and the objections of Herero and Nama representatives. The act ends on a question still open between governments and descendant communities.

## Comparative note

The Congo Free State (Episode 29) killed through a revenue system. Southwest Africa included an explicit annihilation order. Historians debate links between colonial violence and later Nazi crimes (the “continuity thesis”). The series presents that debate as a debate.

## How we know

German military and colonial records (Bundesarchiv; National Archives of Namibia). The British *Blue Book* of 1918 on Southwest Africa, a wartime document later suppressed, labeled as partisan and evidentiary at once. Herero and Nama oral histories, with consultation. Scholarship by Jürgen Zimmerer, Isabel Hull (*Absolute Destruction*, 2005), Jan-Bart Gewald, and on Maji Maji by John Iliffe.

## Do not claim

- That the Herero and Nama did not fight; they waged organized war.
- That death figures are exact; they are estimates.
- That the 2021 declaration settled the matter; descendant communities dispute it.
- That German colonialism was short and therefore minor.
- That a direct line from Namibia to the Holocaust is established; it is debated.
