## Before

Season 3 filmed Britain. Other empires followed other paths. The Dutch Republic, fighting Spain for independence, sent fleets to Asia in the 1590s. In 1602 the States General merged competing companies into one: the VOC.

## The turn

The VOC’s charter gave it the right to make war, build forts, and sign treaties east of the Cape of Good Hope. It pursued monopoly by force. In the Moluccas it sought exclusive control of cloves. The Banda Islands were then the world’s only source of nutmeg and mace. Their inhabitants traded with many buyers and resisted Dutch monopoly contracts. In 1621 Governor-General Jan Pieterszoon Coen led an expedition that killed, enslaved, deported, or drove into flight most of the Bandanese population. Estimates suggest a population of around 15,000 fell to perhaps a thousand remaining on the islands. The near-destruction is evidenced; the numbers are estimates. The company then divided the land into nutmeg plantations (*perken*) worked by enslaved people under Dutch *perkeniers*.

Coen founded Batavia (Jakarta) in 1619 on the ruins of Jayakarta. It became the VOC’s Asian headquarters, a city of Chinese, Javanese, enslaved people from across the Indian Ocean, and a small Dutch elite. In 1740 the VOC and its allies massacred Batavia’s Chinese residents; estimates run to about 10,000 dead. In 1652 Jan van Riebeeck set up a refreshment station at the Cape for passing ships. It expanded onto Khoekhoe land and became a settler colony dependent on enslaved people brought from Asia and Africa. In the Atlantic, the Dutch West India Company held New Amsterdam from the 1620s until England took it in 1664.

## After

Episode 26 moves to Java in the 19th century, after the company had collapsed and the Dutch state had taken over.

## Acts

### 1. A company at war

The 1602 charter, the VOC’s fleets, and its early wars with the Portuguese and with Asian rulers. Monopoly was a military strategy, not a market outcome.

### 2. Banda, 1621

The Bandanese as traders who dealt with many buyers. Coen’s instructions and the 1621 expedition. The near-destruction stated once, with the estimate and its source. No massacre montage; the camera stays on documents, the islands, and the Bandanese diaspora in the Kei Islands, whose descendants keep memory and a Bandanese language.

### 3. Batavia

The city built over Jayakarta. Chinese residents as essential and feared. The 1740 massacre named with its estimated toll and its causes. Enslaved people from South Asia, Bali, Sulawesi, and elsewhere as a large share of the population.

### 4. The Cape

Van Riebeeck’s station, the Khoekhoe wars (1659, 1673–77), and the smallpox epidemic of 1713. Enslaved people from Madagascar, India, and the Indonesian archipelago. The shift from station to settler colony, handed to Episode 54.

### 5. New Amsterdam and the company’s end

The WIC in North America and its purchase claims from the Lenape. The loss to England in 1664. The VOC’s bankruptcy and dissolution (1799). The state inherits its territories. Coen’s statue in Hoorn and the debate over it in the Netherlands.

## Comparative note

The English East India Company (Episodes 17, 21) was slower to seek territorial monopoly by conquest. The Dutch and English companies were the same form used under different conditions, and neither is ranked morally.

## How we know

VOC archives in the Nationaal Archief (The Hague) and the Arsip Nasional Republik Indonesia (Jakarta), inscribed together on UNESCO’s Memory of the World register. Coen’s correspondence. Bandanese oral tradition in the Kei Islands, labeled as later memory. Scholarship by Vincent Loth, Willard Hanna, and Marjolein ’t Hart; the Encompass project’s work on VOC archives.

## Do not claim

- That the Bandanese population figures are exact; they are estimates.
- That Coen alone was responsible; the company’s directors endorsed monopoly by force.
- That the Cape was empty; it was Khoekhoe and San land.
- That the VOC was a peaceful trading company.
- That the Bandanese people vanished; descendants and memory survive.
