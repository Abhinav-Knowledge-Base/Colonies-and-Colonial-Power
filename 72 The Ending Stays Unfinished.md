## Before

Seventy-one episodes followed ships, companies, plantations, missions, law, guns, and the people who survived and ended much of it. Episode 71 showed what the treaties left. This hour asks what is still unfinished.

## The turn

Settler states did not “leave.” The United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and others are states built on Indigenous land, and Indigenous nations within them continue to press sovereignty and land claims through courts and politics. Australia’s High Court rejected *terra nullius* in *Mabo* (1992). The Waitangi Tribunal hears Māori claims. The US Supreme Court held in *McGirt v. Oklahoma* (2020) that the Muscogee (Creek) reservation had never been disestablished. The UN adopted the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples in 2007. Four settler states voted against it at first and later endorsed it.

Apartheid ended with South Africa’s first democratic election in 1994. That did not mean colonialism had ended everywhere, and it did not settle land ownership in South Africa, which remains a live political question. Seventeen territories remain on the UN list of non-self-governing territories, including Western Sahara, New Caledonia (which held independence referendums in 2018, 2020, and 2021, and saw unrest in 2024), and several Caribbean and Pacific islands. Puerto Rico remains a US territory. The Chagos Islands, separated from Mauritius by Britain in 1965 and cleared of their inhabitants, were the subject of an International Court of Justice advisory opinion in 2019, and Britain and Mauritius announced an agreement on sovereignty in 2024.

People migrate from former colonies to former imperial centers, following language and history. The Windrush scandal in Britain (2018) exposed how people invited from the Caribbean after 1948 were later treated. Climate change raises questions about colonial land use: plantation monocultures, deforestation, extraction, and who bears the costs of a warming world. The series presents that as a question, not a slogan.

## After

There is no next episode. The series ends with a list.

## Acts

### 1. Settler states still there

Indigenous sovereignty cases: *Mabo*, the Waitangi Tribunal, *McGirt*, UNDRIP. Indigenous nations speaking for themselves, with consent.

### 2. 1994 is not the end

South Africa’s transition and its unfinished land question. The non-self-governing territories, New Caledonia, Western Sahara, Puerto Rico, and the Chagos Islands. Colonialism in the present tense.

### 3. Migration back

Postwar migration to Britain, France, the Netherlands, and Portugal. The Windrush scandal. Former colonial subjects as citizens and as targets of exclusion.

### 4. Land, climate, and a question

Colonial land use — plantations, mines, forests cleared for export crops — and its ecological legacies. Climate responsibility and vulnerability, presented as a question with evidence on several sides, not a slogan.

### 5. What the series still owes

A closing list on screen: the counts of the dead that cannot be settled; enslaved and colonized women’s lives the archive did not record; inland societies with thin European sources; the voices of the enslaved on the ships; Indigenous histories held in languages and oral traditions the series could only glimpse; the many clocks of decolonization still running. The final shot returns to the cold open’s border that does not move. Title.

## Comparative note

The series began with a Roman word (Episode 1) and ends with states and territories whose borders and sovereignty are still contested. Colonial power was a world system. Its afterlives are also global, and they are not the same everywhere.

## How we know

Court decisions and tribunal reports. UN records on non-self-governing territories and UNDRIP. The ICJ advisory opinion (2019). Government inquiries (the Windrush Lessons Learned Review, 2020). Climate science and environmental history, labeled by their evidence. Indigenous and descendant community testimony, with consent.

## Do not claim

- That colonialism ended in 1975, 1994, or any single year.
- That settler states have decolonized.
- That South Africa’s 1994 transition means colonialism ended everywhere.
- That climate change can be assigned to colonialism alone; it is a question about responsibility and land use.
- That the series has told the whole story; it lists what it owes.
